F.R.E.E.D.O.M. is two frameworks sharing one acronym. That is intentional.

The first is a diagnostic — seven domains we score every business across to find the highest-leverage fix point. The second is a methodology — seven steps we move every engagement through, from filter (qualify the operator) to maximize (compound the system). Together they answer the two questions every operator engagement starts with: *where is the constraint, and how do we install the fix?*

## Part 1 — The 7-Domain Diagnostic

Each domain is scored from a structured 24-question assessment. The lowest-scoring domain is the highest-leverage fix. Financial Foundations is first because no later domain works if the financials are unclear.

### F — Financial Foundations
Is the business profitable enough to fund its own growth? Sub-factors: margin health, cash flow and runway, revenue trajectory, pricing architecture, customer concentration. **What good looks like:** monthly P&L by product/service line, cash position visible in real time, no customer >15–20% of revenue, pricing reviewed and documented annually.

### R — Revenue Systems
Can the sales engine run without the founder pushing every deal? Sub-factors: sales process definition, pipeline discipline, lead generation infrastructure, conversion systems, scalable acquisition. **What good looks like:** documented multi-stage sales process, weekly pipeline reviews, at least one acquisition channel that produces leads without the founder posting personally.

### E — Execution Engine
Does the team execute consistently? Sub-factors: accountability rhythm, weekly meeting cadence, 90-day Rocks alignment, scorecard discipline. **What good looks like:** weekly meeting cadence with a written agenda and accountability for outcomes, 90-day Rocks for every team member, scorecards reviewed weekly.

### E — Employee & Team Excellence
Is the org structure built for growth — or built around the founder? Sub-factors: org clarity, role definition, owner extraction plan, delegation maturity, hiring & compensation alignment. **What good looks like:** every role has a scorecard, founder's calendar is <30% reactive, compensation tied to outcomes, hiring uses a documented scorecard.

### D — Data & Decisions
Single source of truth — or the inbox? Sub-factors: tech stack efficiency, CRM reliability, reporting infrastructure, data integration, decision quality. **What good looks like:** automated dashboards, decisions logged, tech stack consolidated, every leader pulling from the same data set.

### O — Operations & Optimization
Are processes documented, repeatable, and improvable? Sub-factors: systems documentation, automation opportunities, playbook maturity, process consistency, operational efficiency. **What good looks like:** documented playbooks per function reviewed quarterly, onboarding from documentation not founder memory, automation prioritized.

### M — Marketing & Market Position
Does the market know who you are and why you win? Sub-factors: brand clarity, positioning strength, content engine, market awareness, competitive differentiation. **What good looks like:** written positioning, documented ICP, content engine that runs without the founder writing every post, sales team can articulate differentiation cleanly.

## Part 2 — The 7-Step Engagement Methodology

The diagnostic tells us what to fix. The methodology tells us how to engage.

1. **F — Filter:** Qualify the operator and the opportunity. Not every business is a fit. The diagnostic is the filter.
2. **R — Relate:** Establish operator-to-operator credibility. Find the language and the pain that match this specific business.
3. **E — Educate:** Teach the operating principles and the diagnostic results. The operator must understand the system to defend it later.
4. **E — Empower:** Hand over tools, scorecards, and language. The team starts speaking the same operating language.
5. **D — Define:** Write the playbooks. Document the SOPs. Lock in role scorecards and KPIs.
6. **O — Optimize:** Run the system. Iterate weekly and monthly. Tighten where leak persists.
7. **M — Maximize:** Compound the system. Layer growth, hiring, and acquisition on top of a stable operating base.

By the time an engagement reaches Maximize, the operator should be running the system on their own — with playbooks, scorecards, and dashboards installed across all seven domains.

Run the [FREEDOM Diagnostic](https://preview--mmldiagnostic.lovable.app/). Read your scores in order — Financial Foundations first, Marketing & Market Position last. The lowest score is where you have the most leverage. Don't try to fix everything. Fix the constraint. The next constraint will surface on its own.
