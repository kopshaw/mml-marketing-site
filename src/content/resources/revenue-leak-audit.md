Most operators look for revenue growth in the wrong place. They look for new customers when the bigger leverage is in revenue they're already earning — and silently leaking.

The four predictable leaks in $1M–$10M businesses:

## Leak 1 — Pricing

Service businesses that haven't reviewed pricing in 12+ months are almost always leaving 10–25% margin on the table. The pattern: prices were set when the business was less capable, the offer has improved, the cost to deliver has gone up, and the price has stayed the same.

**Audit:**

- When was the last price increase? If >12 months ago, leak likely.
- What is the price relative to direct competitors and adjacent alternatives?
- What is the cost to deliver, and what is the gross margin? If margin is below 50% on a service line, pricing or scope is wrong.
- Has the offer materially improved since the last price set?

**Fix:** annual pricing review locked into the operating cadence. Most operators recover 5–15% of revenue from a single disciplined pricing pass.

## Leak 2 — Silent customer churn

Customers who quietly stop renewing, downgrade, or stop spending without formally churning. Most service businesses don't track this because they don't have a churn metric — they only notice when revenue drops three months later.

**Audit:**

- What's your monthly customer churn rate? If you don't know, leak likely.
- For your top 20% of customers (revenue), what's the trend on monthly spend over the last 12 months? Declining or flat = leak.
- Do you have a defined process for at-risk customers? If no, leak.

**Fix:** monthly churn tracking, defined at-risk triggers, named owner for retention. The Revenue Systems domain in [F.R.E.E.D.O.M.](/resources/freedom-framework) covers this directly.

## Leak 3 — Pipeline conversion drop-off

Most service businesses have stage drop-offs they can't see because their pipeline isn't documented stage-by-stage. Leads that should close don't, and the operator doesn't know which stage is leaking.

**Audit:**

- Is your sales process documented stage-by-stage with conversion rates per stage? If no, leak likely.
- What's your typical close rate from qualified lead to closed-won? Industry benchmarks: 15–30% for most service businesses.
- Where in the funnel are you losing the most volume? If you don't know, leak.

**Fix:** documented sales process, weekly pipeline review, conversion rate tracked per stage. See [How to Build a Sales Playbook](/resources/sales-playbook).

## Leak 4 — Unbilled work

Overdelivery, scope creep, missed billables, work performed but never invoiced. Common in agency, consulting, and professional services. Often 5–10% of total work hours.

**Audit:**

- For project-based work: are project hours tracked against budget? Are overages invoiced?
- For retainer work: is the scope of the retainer documented in a written SOW reviewed quarterly?
- For one-off requests from existing clients: is there a process for invoicing add-on work?

**Fix:** time tracking against scope, formal change order process, quarterly retainer scope review. The MML Toolbox includes a Revenue Leak Finder Scorecard with a 40-point checklist covering these.

## Total leak math

For a typical $3M service business with all four leaks present:

| Leak | Recoverable | At $3M revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | 5–10% | $150K–$300K |
| Silent churn | 2–5% | $60K–$150K |
| Pipeline conversion | 3–7% | $90K–$210K |
| Unbilled work | 2–5% | $60K–$150K |
| **Total** | **12–27%** | **$360K–$810K** |

That is more leverage than most growth campaigns produce.
