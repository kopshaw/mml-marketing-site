A scorecard exists for one reason: to answer *"is the right person in this seat?"* with data instead of feeling.

Without a scorecard, that question gets answered by the founder's gut every quarter. Sometimes the answer is right; sometimes it's wrong; in either case it's not auditable, and it tends to surface only when frustration is already high.

With a scorecard, the question is answered by the numbers every week. The team member knows whether they're winning. The founder knows whether the seat is filled. Hard conversations happen earlier, calmly, on data.

## How to build one in 30 minutes

### Step 1 — Identify the outputs (10 min)

Don't list responsibilities. List measurable outputs. The difference matters: *"Manage the sales pipeline"* is a responsibility. *"Pipeline value at end of week ≥ $X"* is an output.

Pick 3–7. Fewer is better than more. Common output types:

- **Volume:** how much (calls, leads, hires, deliverables)
- **Quality:** how well (NPS, conversion rate, error rate)
- **Velocity:** how fast (cycle time, time to close, time to hire)
- **Financial:** what it's worth (revenue closed, margin generated, cost per acquisition)

### Step 2 — Set the weekly target (10 min)

Each output gets a weekly target. The target should be:

- Measurable from a real data source (not a feeling)
- Achievable but not trivial (the team member should be able to hit it most weeks if performing)
- Owned by the role, not split across multiple people

If you can't measure it from a real data source, change the output. Outputs that can't be measured are responsibilities, not scorecards.

### Step 3 — Set the review cadence (10 min)

The number gets reviewed weekly at the leadership meeting. Show the number. Show the target. The only question is *"on track or off track?"*

If on track: 30 seconds, move on.
If off track: it becomes an issue on the issues list. The team member owns the analysis. Long conversation happens outside the meeting.

## What a working scorecard looks like

| Output | Weekly target | This week | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified leads created | 25 | 27 | ✓ |
| Discovery calls held | 15 | 16 | ✓ |
| Proposals sent | 8 | 6 | ✗ |
| Closed-won revenue | $20K | $18K | ✗ |
| Customer NPS (rolling) | 50+ | 58 | ✓ |

Two off-track. Two issues to add to the list. 30 seconds of meeting time.

## The mistake to avoid

The most common mistake is to skip the weekly review. Scorecards that aren't reviewed weekly become decorative. Decorative scorecards become founder dependency again — because the only person who knows whether the role is on track is the founder, who is reviewing in their head.

Review weekly. Same time, same agenda, same scorecard format. The discipline is the system.
