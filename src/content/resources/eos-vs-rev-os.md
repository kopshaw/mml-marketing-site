EOS and Rev OS are often compared because both promise an "operating system" for your business. They solve overlapping problems, but they aren't the same thing — and the difference matters.

## What EOS is

The Entrepreneurial Operating System, from Gino Wickman's book *Traction*, is a meeting and accountability framework. The core components:

- **Vision/Traction Organizer (V/TO):** strategic plan on two pages
- **Rocks:** 90-day priorities per leader
- **Level 10 Meeting:** weekly meeting structure with a fixed agenda
- **Scorecard:** 5–15 weekly numbers reviewed at the L10
- **People Analyzer / GWC:** hiring framework (Get it, Want it, Capacity)
- **Issues List:** running log of problems to solve

EOS is genuinely good at what it does. The L10 meeting structure is one of the most well-engineered tools in operating literature.

## What the Rev OS adds

The Rev OS — installed by Metric Mentor Labs — covers seven domains:

| Domain | EOS | Rev OS |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting cadence & accountability | ✓ (L10) | ✓ |
| 90-day priorities | ✓ (Rocks) | ✓ |
| Hiring framework | ✓ (People Analyzer) | ✓ |
| Financial dashboard & KPI tree | Partial | ✓ |
| Documented sales process & pipeline | — | ✓ |
| Single source of truth dashboard | — | ✓ |
| Documented SOPs per function | — | ✓ |
| Marketing positioning & content engine | — | ✓ |
| Pricing architecture & margin discipline | — | ✓ |
| Customer concentration & cash discipline | — | ✓ |

The seven domains in F.R.E.E.D.O.M. — Financial Foundations, Revenue Systems, Execution Engine, Employee & Team Excellence, Data & Decisions, Operations & Optimization, and Marketing & Market Position — span infrastructure that EOS does not directly address.

## When EOS is enough

EOS is often the right answer when:

- The team is mostly aligned and effective, but accountability and meeting cadence are weak
- The business already has financial visibility and a working sales process
- The constraint is execution rhythm, not financial discipline or marketing positioning

For many $1M–$3M businesses, EOS — properly implemented by a certified Implementer — is the highest-leverage fix.

## When the Rev OS is the better fit

The Rev OS tends to be the right answer when:

- Multiple domains are weak simultaneously — e.g., shaky financials AND undocumented sales process AND inconsistent positioning
- The business is preparing for a capital event (raise, acquisition, exit) and needs broader infrastructure
- The founder needs an embedded executive (Fractional Growth Leadership) running the system, not just an Implementer auditing meetings

## They are not mutually exclusive

The Execution Engine domain in F.R.E.E.D.O.M. *includes* meeting cadence and accountability rhythm — and Rev OS engagements often use L10-style meetings as the working format. The two systems are compatible; the Rev OS is broader.
