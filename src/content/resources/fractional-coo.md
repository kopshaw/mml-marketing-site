The fractional COO model exists because most $1M–$10M businesses have a problem they can't afford to solve.

The problem: the founder is the bottleneck for operations, hiring, and execution. The business has outgrown one-person leadership.

The constraint: a full-time COO at $150K–$300K all-in is unaffordable, premature, or both. Hiring one means betting the business can support that overhead before you've proven the operating model can scale.

The fractional COO bridges the gap. Senior operating capacity, embedded in the business, owning the outcomes — without the full-time payroll commitment.

## What a fractional COO actually does

The work is operating, not advising. A fractional COO:

- Owns the operating cadence — weekly leadership meeting, monthly review, quarterly Rocks
- Builds and maintains the role scorecards — every key seat gets a documented scorecard
- Hires and manages — interviews, onboards, fires when needed
- Runs the financial reporting rhythm — monthly P&L review, margin tracking, KPI tree
- Owns the sales operating process — pipeline reviews, conversion analysis, accountability
- Documents the SOPs — turns founder knowledge into team-runnable playbooks
- Reports to the founder/board — quarterly outcomes, monthly progress, weekly status

The role is functional. They show up. They run things. They are responsible for quarterly outcomes. They are not a consultant with opinions — they are an operator with accountability.

## When the fractional model works (and when it doesn't)

**It works when:**

- The business is doing $1M–$25M in real revenue
- The founder is the named bottleneck for ops, hiring, or strategy
- The team can support an embedded executive (3+ people minimum)
- There is at least one repeatable revenue motion to scale
- The founder is willing to delegate real authority, not just task lists

**It does not work when:**

- The business is pre-revenue or sub-$500K — there is no operation to run yet
- The founder wants help but won't actually delegate decisions
- The company is mid-crisis with cash runway under 60 days — that's a turnaround, not a fractional engagement
- The business has no documented offers, customers, or model — the work is product-market fit, not operations

## The cost math

| Option | Annual cost (all-in) | Hours/week |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time COO | $200K–$400K | 40+ |
| Fractional COO at $5K/mo | $60K | 10–15 |
| Fractional COO at $10K/mo | $120K | 20–25 |
| Fractional Growth Leadership at $15K/mo | $180K | 25–30 |

Most $1M–$10M businesses get the senior capacity they need at $5K–$10K/month. By the time they need 30+ hours per week of executive operating capacity, they are usually at the scale to justify hiring full-time — and a well-run fractional engagement creates the seat, the scorecard, and the playbook for that hire.
