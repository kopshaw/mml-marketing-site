Most operators trying to scale past $1M assume they need more demand.

They almost never do. They are not demand-constrained. Somebody is buying. The plateau is showing up because the business has scaled to the limit of *one person's executable capacity* — and it cannot grow past that limit until the next layer of infrastructure is installed.

## The four required pieces

### 1. A documented sales process that someone else can run

If every deal is a custom build, every quarter is a guess. The fix: write down the stages, the qualifying criteria at each stage, the questions, the objection handling, the close. Not as a manual — as a working playbook a teammate uses Monday morning.

The test: can a part-time or fractional salesperson read the document and close a deal at your typical conversion rate? If no, the process isn't documented yet.

### 2. One repeatable acquisition channel that doesn't depend on you personally

If the lead pipeline depends on the founder posting on social, attending events, or working personal network, the business can't grow past the founder's calendar.

The fix: build at least one acquisition channel that produces leads automatically — paid ads, referral system, partnership funnel, content engine. Doesn't have to be many. Has to be one that runs without the founder doing the work.

### 3. Role scorecards for delivery

Quality variance kills businesses scaling past $1M. Same role, different humans, wildly different output — and the founder can't be in every delivery to enforce quality.

The fix: every delivery role gets a written scorecard with measurable outputs. The scorecard is reviewed weekly. The team member is responsible for hitting the score, not for being told they're doing well.

### 4. Monthly financial close

You cannot make growth decisions from a bank balance. You need monthly P&L by service line, gross margin, customer concentration, and cash position. Most $500K–$1M businesses don't have this. Most $1M+ businesses that broke through do.

## The order matters

Run the FREEDOM Diagnostic to see which of the four is your specific constraint. The lowest-scoring domain is the highest-leverage fix point. Most operators come in thinking they need leads (Marketing) and discover the constraint is actually Financial Foundations or Revenue Systems.

Don't try to install all four at once. Pick the highest-leverage gap. Install it. Prove it for one quarter. Then move to the next.

The ceiling breaks when the constraint is removed — not when more effort is applied to the wrong layer.
