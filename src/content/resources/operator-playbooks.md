Most operators try to document their business the wrong way: they sit down on a Saturday, open a blank doc, and try to write everything they know.

This never works. By Monday the document is incomplete, the founder is back in execution mode, and the business is no further along.

The right way is the opposite: document the work while it's happening.

## The 5-step playbook for building playbooks

### 1. Pick by leverage, not by completeness

Don't try to document everything. Pick the highest-frequency, highest-cost task that currently depends on the founder. For most $500K–$5M businesses that's one of:

- Client onboarding
- Sales call to close
- Weekly leadership meeting
- Monthly financial close
- Quarterly hiring sequence

Start there. Document one. Ship it. Move to the next.

### 2. Capture, don't compose

The fastest way to capture an SOP is to record yourself doing the work once, in real time, narrating what you're doing as you do it. Loom, screen recorder, voice memo — whatever is fastest. Transcribe with Otter, Whisper, or any transcription tool. The transcription becomes the rough draft of the SOP.

You will write a worse SOP from a blank page than you will edit from a transcript of yourself doing the work. Trust this.

### 3. Structure as numbered steps with explicit decisions

Convert the transcript into numbered steps. Every step is one verb. Every decision point gets explicit language: *"If X, do A. If Y, do B. If neither, escalate to [name]."*

The thing that makes an SOP usable is not how thorough it is — it's how unambiguous the decision points are. A 1-page SOP with clear decisions beats a 10-page SOP with vague guidance.

### 4. Test by handing it to the next person

Don't trust your own SOP. Hand it to the next team member who needs to execute the task and watch them try to follow it. Wherever they get confused, ask, or come back to you — that's a gap. Fix the gap. Re-test.

The SOP is done when a competent team member can execute it correctly *without asking you a question*.

### 5. Lock it into the quarterly operating rhythm

Every quarter, every active SOP gets a 15-minute review with the person executing it. Two questions: *what broke?* and *what could be tighter?* Update the doc. Note the date.

SOPs that aren't reviewed go stale. Stale SOPs get ignored. Ignored SOPs become founder dependencies again.

## What goes in every SOP

Five elements:

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | When does this SOP start? (Event, calendar, threshold, request) |
| **Inputs** | What do you need to have on hand to begin? (Data, access, tools) |
| **Steps** | Numbered, one-verb steps with explicit decision points |
| **Outputs** | What does "done" look like? (Deliverable, communication, system update) |
| **Escalation** | What if the SOP doesn't cover this case? Who do you escalate to? |

Keep it under two pages where possible. If an SOP needs to be longer, break it into linked sub-SOPs.

## The 8–15 SOPs every $500K–$5M business needs

For most service businesses:

1. Client onboarding (from contract signed to first deliverable)
2. Sales process by stage (lead → qualified → proposal → close)
3. Weekly leadership meeting structure
4. Monthly financial close
5. Quarterly planning rhythm
6. Hiring sequence (job posting → offer)
7. Onboarding for new team members
8. Billing and invoicing
9. Client offboarding / renewal
10. Performance review cadence
11. Marketing content production
12. Customer support escalation
13. Quarterly SOP review (the meta-playbook)

Plus one playbook per repeatable service offering you sell.

That's it. That's most of the leverage.
