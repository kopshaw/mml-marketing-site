Most operators between $500K and $25M can name their revenue. Few can name the five numbers that drive their revenue.

That gap is the constraint. You can't run a business by data you don't track.

## The five core KPIs

### 1. Gross margin by service line

Not blended gross margin. By service line. Most service businesses have one or two lines that fund growth and one or two lines that consume it — and most operators don't know which is which because the margin is averaged.

**How to track:** monthly P&L with revenue and direct cost broken out per service line. Calculate gross margin = (revenue − direct cost) / revenue.

**Why it matters:** the line with the highest margin should get the most growth investment. The line with the lowest margin should be repriced, restructured, or retired.

### 2. Customer acquisition cost (blended)

The all-in cost to acquire one new customer, including paid ads, content, sales team time, and any partner or referral commissions.

**How to track:** monthly. Total marketing + sales spend ÷ new customers acquired = blended CAC.

**Why it matters:** this is the unit economics number. Without it you cannot evaluate whether to scale acquisition, kill a channel, or shift budget.

### 3. Lifetime value

The total gross profit you expect to earn from one customer over their full lifecycle.

**How to track:** quarterly (it doesn't move week to week). Average customer revenue × average gross margin × average customer lifespan.

**Why it matters:** the LTV / CAC ratio tells you whether your acquisition is profitable. Healthy services target ≥3:1; SaaS-style targets ≥4:1. Below 2:1 means you are acquiring at a loss.

### 4. Monthly recurring or contracted revenue

The portion of revenue you can count on next month based on existing contracts, subscriptions, or retainers.

**How to track:** weekly snapshot, monthly close. Sum of all active recurring or contracted commitments.

**Why it matters:** this is your predictable base. Total revenue minus recurring is project / one-time revenue, which has different operating characteristics. Most operators dramatically overestimate their predictable base.

### 5. Cash runway

The number of months the business can operate at current burn before cash runs out.

**How to track:** monthly. Current cash position ÷ monthly net burn = runway in months.

**Why it matters:** this is the only number that determines whether you have time. Below 6 months: turnaround mode. 6–12 months: growth mode with discipline. 12+ months: investment mode.

## Secondary metrics

These explain movement in the core five but aren't replacements for them:

- Pipeline value, conversion rate, sales velocity → explain CAC and recurring revenue movement
- Customer NPS, churn rate, expansion revenue → explain LTV movement
- Operating expense ratio, cost of delivery → explain margin movement
- Days sales outstanding, accounts payable timing → explain cash runway movement

Track them. But don't confuse them with the core five.

## The review cadence

| KPI | Cadence | Where it's reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline value & conversion | Weekly | Leadership meeting |
| Gross margin (blended + by line) | Monthly | Financial close |
| CAC | Monthly | Financial close |
| Cash position & runway | Monthly | Financial close |
| Recurring revenue | Monthly | Financial close |
| LTV | Quarterly | Quarterly review |
| Customer concentration | Quarterly | Quarterly review |

The cadence matters as much as the metric. KPIs that aren't reviewed on a defined rhythm are decorative.
